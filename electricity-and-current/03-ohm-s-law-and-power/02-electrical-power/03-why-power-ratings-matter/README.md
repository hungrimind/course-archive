# Why Power Ratings Matter

Every component has a **maximum power rating** -- exceed it and the component overheats. A common small resistor is rated for just **0.25W**.

> [!warning]
> If your circuit pushes 0.5W through a 0.25W resistor, it will get dangerously hot and could burn up.

Always calculate the power a component will handle (P = V x I) and make sure it stays well under the rating. *This is the difference between a circuit that works and one that lets out the magic smoke.*

## Ohm's Law Practice

**1.** Given voltage = 5, current = 0.04, solve for **power** (W).
Hint: P = V × I

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

0.2 W

5V × 0.04A = 0.2W — safe for a 0.25W resistor

</details>

</details>
