# What Is Power?

**Power** measures how fast electrical energy is consumed. It's measured in **watts (W)** -- one watt means one joule of energy used every second.

A 60W lightbulb burns through energy six times faster than a 10W one. *More watts doesn't mean "more electricity" exactly -- it means energy is being used up more quickly.*

## True or False

**1. True or false:** A 100W lightbulb uses more total energy than a 10W lightbulb no matter what.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: Power measures the rate of energy use, not total energy. A 10W bulb left on for 100 hours uses more total energy than a 100W bulb on for 5 minutes. Total energy depends on both power and time.

</details>

**2. True or false:** One watt equals one joule of energy consumed per second.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: By definition, 1 watt = 1 joule/second. Watts measure how quickly energy is being used.

</details>

**3. True or false:** A device with a higher wattage rating consumes energy at a faster rate.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: Higher watts means more joules used per second, so yes, energy is consumed more quickly.

</details>

**4. True or false:** Power is measured in volts.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: Power is measured in watts (W). Volts measure voltage, which is electrical pressure -- a different quantity entirely.

</details>
