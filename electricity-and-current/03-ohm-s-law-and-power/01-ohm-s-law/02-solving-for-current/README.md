# Solving for Current

Rearrange Ohm's Law to find current: **I = V / R**. Divide the voltage by the resistance and you get the current flowing through the circuit.

Say you have 9V across a 1,000-ohm resistor. That's 9 / 1000 = **0.009A**, or **9mA**. The higher the resistance, the less current gets through -- *resistance is literally resisting the flow*.

## Ohm's Law Practice

**1.** Given voltage = 9, resistance = 1000, solve for **current** (A).
Hint: I = V ÷ R

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

0.009 A

9V ÷ 1000Ω = 0.009A (9mA)

</details>

**2.** Given voltage = 3.3, resistance = 220, solve for **current** (A).
Hint: I = V ÷ R

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

0.015 A

3.3V ÷ 220Ω = 0.015A (15mA)

</details>

**3.** Given voltage = 12, resistance = 4700, solve for **current** (A).
Hint: I = V ÷ R

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

0.00255 A

12V ÷ 4700Ω ≈ 0.00255A (2.55mA)

</details>
