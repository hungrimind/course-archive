# Short Circuits

A **short circuit** happens when current finds a shortcut -- bypassing components and flowing straight from positive to negative with almost no resistance. With nothing to slow it down, current surges and wires heat up dangerously fast.

That's why we have **fuses** and **circuit breakers**. They monitor current flow and cut the connection the moment it spikes too high, breaking the loop before anything melts or catches fire.

## Quiz

**1. What happens during a short circuit?**

- A) Current flows normally through all components
- B) Current bypasses components and flows with almost no resistance
- C) The circuit opens and current stops flowing
- D) Voltage drops to zero across all components

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) Current bypasses components and flows with almost no resistance

A short circuit occurs when current finds a shortcut that bypasses components, flowing straight from positive to negative with almost no resistance, causing a dangerous surge.

</details>

**2. What is the purpose of fuses and circuit breakers?**

- A) They increase the voltage in a circuit
- B) They store extra current for later use
- C) They cut the connection when current spikes too high
- D) They redirect current to bypass damaged components

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) They cut the connection when current spikes too high

Fuses and circuit breakers monitor current flow and break the circuit the moment current spikes dangerously high, preventing wires from overheating, melting, or catching fire.

</details>
