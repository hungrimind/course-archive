# How Batteries Work

A battery is a tiny chemical factory. Inside, two different materials react with each other, and that reaction *shoves electrons* out of the negative terminal. Those electrons travel through your circuit doing useful work, then return through the positive terminal to complete the loop.

The reaction keeps going until the chemicals are used up -- that is when your battery "dies." Rechargeable batteries can run the reaction *in reverse* when you plug them in, restoring the chemicals for another round.

## True or False

**1. True or false:** A battery produces electricity through a chemical reaction between two different materials inside it.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: Batteries contain two different materials that react chemically, and this reaction pushes electrons out of the negative terminal.

</details>

**2. True or false:** When a battery dies, the electrons inside it have been used up.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: Electrons are not consumed. A battery dies when the chemicals that drive the reaction are used up, not the electrons themselves.

</details>

**3. True or false:** Rechargeable batteries restore their chemicals by running the internal reaction in reverse.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: Plugging in a rechargeable battery forces the chemical reaction to run backward, restoring the original chemicals so the battery can discharge again.

</details>

**4. True or false:** Electrons exit a battery through the positive terminal and return through the negative terminal.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: Electrons exit through the negative terminal, travel through the circuit, and return through the positive terminal.

</details>
