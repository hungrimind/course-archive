# What Is DC?

**Direct current (DC)** is electricity that flows in *one direction only*, like water flowing down a river. The electrons leave the battery's negative terminal, travel through the circuit, and return to the positive terminal in a steady, constant stream.

Because the flow never reverses, the voltage stays at a fixed level. A 9V battery always pushes at 9 volts -- no surprises, no back-and-forth.

## True or False

**1. True or false:** In a DC circuit, electrons periodically reverse direction to balance the flow.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: DC stands for direct current, meaning electrons flow in one direction only and never reverse.

</details>

**2. True or false:** A 9V battery provides a constant voltage that does not fluctuate up and down.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: DC delivers a fixed, steady voltage level. A 9V battery consistently pushes at 9 volts.

</details>

**3. True or false:** In DC, electrons travel from the positive terminal through the circuit to the negative terminal.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: Electrons flow from the negative terminal through the circuit and return to the positive terminal.

</details>

**4. True or false:** The flow of DC electricity can be compared to water flowing steadily down a river.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: Like water in a river that moves in one consistent direction, DC electricity flows one way without reversing.

</details>
