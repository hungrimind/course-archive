# What Is AC?

**Alternating current (AC)** switches direction back and forth many times per second. Unlike DC's steady river, AC electrons *wiggle in place* -- they do not actually travel far down the wire.

Think of a long line of people passing a ball back and forth. Nobody moves, but the energy travels down the line. The voltage swings from positive to negative and back again in a smooth wave pattern.

## Quiz

**1. How do electrons behave in an AC circuit?**

- A) They travel in one direction from negative to positive terminal
- B) They wiggle back and forth in place, not traveling far down the wire
- C) They spin in circles inside the wire
- D) They stop moving entirely between cycles

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) They wiggle back and forth in place, not traveling far down the wire

In AC, electrons oscillate back and forth rather than traveling in one direction. The energy moves down the line, but the electrons themselves stay roughly in place.

</details>

**2. What shape does AC voltage follow over time?**

- A) A flat, constant line
- B) A jagged zigzag pattern
- C) A smooth wave pattern swinging between positive and negative
- D) A series of sudden spikes

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) A smooth wave pattern swinging between positive and negative

AC voltage swings smoothly from positive to negative and back again in a continuous wave pattern, unlike DC's flat constant line.

</details>
