# Why AC Won

AC has one killer advantage: you can easily change its voltage using a simple device called a **transformer**. Crank the voltage *way up* for long-distance transmission (high voltage means less energy lost as heat), then step it back down to safe levels at your house.

DC could not do this cheaply in the 1800s, and that single fact decided the famous **"war of currents."** Edison backed DC, but Tesla and Westinghouse backed AC -- and AC powers the grid to this day.

## Quiz

**1. What device allows AC voltage to be easily changed from high to low or low to high?**

- A) Rectifier
- B) Transformer
- C) Capacitor
- D) Resistor

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) Transformer

A transformer is a simple device that can step AC voltage up or down. This ability is AC's key advantage over DC.

</details>

**2. Why is high voltage used for long-distance power transmission?**

- A) High voltage makes electricity travel faster through the wires
- B) High voltage prevents the wires from overheating
- C) High voltage reduces energy lost as heat during transmission
- D) High voltage is required by law for safety reasons

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) High voltage reduces energy lost as heat during transmission

Transmitting at high voltage means less current is needed for the same power, which results in far less energy wasted as heat in the wires.

</details>

**3. Who backed AC in the famous 'war of currents'?**

- A) Edison and Ford
- B) Tesla and Edison
- C) Tesla and Westinghouse
- D) Westinghouse and Edison

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) Tesla and Westinghouse

Nikola Tesla and George Westinghouse championed AC, while Thomas Edison backed DC. AC won because transformers made long-distance transmission practical and affordable.

</details>
