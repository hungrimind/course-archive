# What Makes Electricity Dangerous

It's **current** that kills, not voltage. As little as **0.1 amps** flowing through your heart can be fatal, and that's a tiny amount.

So why do we worry about voltage? Because voltage is the *push*. Your body has natural resistance, and higher voltage can force enough current through that resistance to reach deadly levels. Voltage is the reason current gets in; current is what does the damage.

## True or False

**1. True or false:** Voltage is what actually damages your body when you get an electrical shock.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: It is current (amps) that does the damage, not voltage. Voltage is the push that forces current through your body's resistance, but the current itself causes the harm.

</details>

**2. True or false:** As little as 0.1 amps through the heart can be fatal.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: 0.1 amps is a tiny amount of current, but it is enough to disrupt the heart's electrical signals and cause death.

</details>

**3. True or false:** Higher voltage can force more current through your body's natural resistance.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: Your body has natural resistance. Higher voltage provides a greater push, overcoming that resistance and allowing more current to flow through you.

</details>

**4. True or false:** If the voltage is low enough, no dangerous current can flow through your body regardless of conditions.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: At very low voltages, your body's resistance is high enough that virtually no current can be pushed through. This is why low-voltage hobby electronics are safe to touch.

</details>
