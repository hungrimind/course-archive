# LED Polarity

LEDs only work when connected the right way around. The longer leg is the **anode** (positive side), and the shorter leg is the **cathode** (negative side).

If you mix them up, the LED simply won't light -- no harm done, just flip it around. On a circuit diagram, the flat edge of the LED's plastic casing also marks the cathode, which helps when the legs have already been trimmed.

## True or False

**1. True or false:** The longer leg of an LED is the cathode (negative side).

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: The longer leg is the anode (positive side). The shorter leg is the cathode (negative side).

</details>

**2. True or false:** If you connect an LED backwards, it will be damaged permanently.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: Connecting an LED backwards simply means it won't light up. No harm is done -- just flip it around to fix it.

</details>

**3. True or false:** The flat edge on an LED's plastic casing marks the cathode (negative side).

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: The flat edge on the LED casing is a visual indicator for the cathode, which is helpful when the legs have been trimmed to equal length.

</details>

**4. True or false:** LEDs work regardless of which direction they are connected.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: LEDs only work when connected the correct way around, with the anode to positive and cathode to negative. They are directional like all diodes.

</details>
