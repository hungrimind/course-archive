# Relays as Switches

A **relay** is a switch controlled by electricity instead of your finger. A small current flows through a coil, creating a magnetic field that pulls a metal contact closed, completing a *separate* circuit.

This separation is the key. A tiny signal from a microcontroller can safely switch on a high-power motor or appliance without the delicate electronics ever touching the dangerous current. It's a bridge between the low-power world of logic and the high-power world of machines.

## True or False

**1. True or false:** A relay is a switch that is controlled by electricity rather than by hand.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: A relay uses a small electrical current through a coil to create a magnetic field that mechanically closes a switch contact, completing a separate circuit.

</details>

**2. True or false:** In a relay, the control signal and the switched circuit share the same current path.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: A key advantage of relays is that the control circuit and the switched circuit are separate. A tiny signal can safely control a high-power circuit without the delicate electronics touching the dangerous current.

</details>

**3. True or false:** A microcontroller can use a relay to safely switch on a high-power appliance.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: Relays allow a small, low-power signal from a microcontroller to control a separate high-power circuit, keeping the delicate electronics safely isolated.

</details>

**4. True or false:** Relays can only switch low-power devices like LEDs.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: Relays are specifically useful for switching high-power devices like motors and appliances. The whole point is that a small signal can control a much larger current.

</details>
