# Buzzers and Sound

A **buzzer** converts electrical signals into sound by vibrating a small disc. The most common type, a **piezo buzzer**, uses a crystal that physically flexes when voltage is applied, pushing air back and forth to create sound waves.

That familiar beep from your microwave, alarm clock, or electronic toy? That's a piezo buzzer doing its thing. Simple, cheap, and loud enough to get your attention -- which is exactly the point.

## True or False

**1. True or false:** A buzzer converts electrical signals into sound by vibrating a small disc.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: Buzzers produce sound by rapidly vibrating a disc, which pushes air back and forth to create sound waves.

</details>

**2. True or false:** A piezo buzzer uses a tiny motor to produce sound.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: A piezo buzzer uses a crystal that physically flexes when voltage is applied, not a motor. This flexing vibrates the disc to produce sound.

</details>

**3. True or false:** The most common type of buzzer is a piezo buzzer.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: Piezo buzzers are the most common type, using a piezoelectric crystal that deforms when voltage is applied to create vibrations and sound.

</details>
