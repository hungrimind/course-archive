# What Makes Electrons Move

Electrons need a push to flow. Without one, they just vibrate randomly in place, with no useful movement.

That push can come from a **battery** (chemical energy), a **generator** (spinning magnets), or even **friction** (rubbing a balloon on your hair). Whatever the source, it creates a difference in charge that shoves electrons in one direction. *No push, no flow.*

## Quiz

**1. What do electrons do when there is no external push applied to them?**

- A) They flow steadily in one direction
- B) They vibrate randomly in place without useful movement
- C) They stop moving entirely and become still
- D) They escape from the atom and float freely

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) They vibrate randomly in place without useful movement

Without a push, electrons just vibrate randomly in place. They don't produce any useful flow. A push is needed to get them moving in a consistent direction.

</details>

**2. Which of the following is NOT mentioned as a source that can push electrons to flow?**

- A) A battery
- B) A generator with spinning magnets
- C) A solar panel absorbing sunlight
- D) Friction from rubbing a balloon on hair

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) A solar panel absorbing sunlight

The lesson mentions three sources that push electrons: batteries (chemical energy), generators (spinning magnets), and friction (like rubbing a balloon on hair). Solar panels were not discussed in this lesson.

</details>
