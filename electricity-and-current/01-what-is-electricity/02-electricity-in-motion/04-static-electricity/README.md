# Static Electricity

**Static electricity** is charge that builds up on a surface and stays put. It doesn't flow through a wire. When you shuffle across carpet, electrons rub off the carpet onto you, giving you an excess negative charge.

Touch a metal doorknob and those extra electrons jump across the gap all at once: *zap*. That tiny spark is static discharge: a sudden rush of electrons finding a path to balance out.

## True or False

**1. True or false:** Static electricity flows continuously through a wire like household electricity does.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: Static electricity is charge that builds up on a surface and stays put. It does not flow through a wire. It discharges all at once in a sudden spark, unlike the continuous flow of current electricity.

</details>

**2. True or false:** When you shuffle across carpet, electrons transfer from the carpet onto you.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: Friction between your feet and the carpet causes electrons to rub off the carpet onto your body, giving you an excess negative charge that discharges when you touch something conductive like a metal doorknob.

</details>

**3. True or false:** A static discharge happens when excess electrons jump across a gap to balance out the charge difference.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: The spark you feel when touching a doorknob is electrons rushing from your body (where they built up) across the gap to the metal, evening out the charge imbalance.

</details>
