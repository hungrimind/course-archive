# What Is a Signal?

A **signal** is information riding on electricity. Instead of just pushing power through a wire, you *vary the voltage over time* to encode a message.

A microphone picks up sound waves and converts them into a changing voltage pattern. A temperature sensor outputs a voltage that rises with heat. In both cases, the electrical pattern *means something*: it carries real-world information from one component to another.

## Quiz

**1. What is a signal in the context of electricity?**

- A) A burst of maximum voltage to power a device
- B) Information encoded by varying voltage over time
- C) A constant voltage used to charge a battery
- D) The physical wire that connects two components

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) Information encoded by varying voltage over time

A signal is not just raw power. It is information riding on electricity. By varying the voltage over time, the electrical pattern encodes a meaningful message that one component can send to another.

</details>

**2. How does a microphone create an electrical signal?**

- A) It generates a fixed voltage whenever sound is present
- B) It stores sound waves in a capacitor for later playback
- C) It converts sound waves into a changing voltage pattern
- D) It amplifies the electricity already flowing through the wire

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) It converts sound waves into a changing voltage pattern

A microphone picks up sound waves and converts them into a continuously changing voltage pattern. That voltage pattern mirrors the shape of the sound, carrying real-world information as an electrical signal.

</details>

**3. A temperature sensor outputs a voltage that rises with heat. What does this voltage represent?**

- A) The amount of current the sensor draws
- B) The resistance of the wire connecting the sensor
- C) Encoded information about the current temperature
- D) A random fluctuation caused by electrical noise

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) Encoded information about the current temperature

The changing voltage from a temperature sensor carries real-world information, specifically the temperature. The voltage pattern means something: as the temperature rises, the voltage rises in a corresponding way.

</details>
