# Measuring Voltage

Voltage is measured in **volts (V)** and is always a difference between two points. You can't measure voltage at a single spot. It's the gap in electrical pressure from one place to another.

A AA battery has 1.5V between its terminals. A car battery has 12V. A wall outlet delivers 120V or 230V depending on your country. The higher the number, the harder the push.

## Quiz

**1. Why can't you measure voltage at a single point?**

- A) Because voltage only exists in AC circuits
- B) Because voltage is the difference in electrical pressure between two points
- C) Because voltmeters require at least three connections
- D) Because voltage disappears when you stop the circuit

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) Because voltage is the difference in electrical pressure between two points

Voltage is always a pressure difference between two points, so you need two reference points to measure it.

</details>

**2. A device operates on two AA batteries wired in series. What total voltage do the batteries provide?**

- A) 1.5V
- B) 0.75V
- C) 3V
- D) 6V

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) 3V

Each AA battery provides 1.5V. In series, voltages add together: 1.5V + 1.5V = 3V.

</details>

**3. Which of the following has the highest voltage?**

- A) A AA battery
- B) A car battery
- C) A European wall outlet
- D) A 9V battery

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) A European wall outlet

A European wall outlet delivers 230V, which is higher than a car battery (12V), a 9V battery, or a AA battery (1.5V).

</details>
