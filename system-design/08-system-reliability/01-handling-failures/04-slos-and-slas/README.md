# SLOs and SLAs

A **Service Level Objective (SLO)** is a measurable target for system performance or reliability, often defined as a percentage. It serves as a benchmark for acceptable service quality.

A **Service Level Agreement (SLA)** is a formal contract that defines the expected service level between a provider and a customer, often including financial penalties for unmet objectives.

> [!info]
> SLOs guide internal goals, while SLAs enforce external commitments. Both are crucial for maintaining trust and ensuring system reliability.

## Quiz

**1. What is a Service Level Objective (SLO)?**

- A) A formal contract defining the expected service level
- B) A measurable target for system performance or reliability
- C) A guideline for financial penalties
- D) A contract ensuring customer service satisfaction

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) A measurable target for system performance or reliability

A Service Level Objective (SLO) is a measurable target, often expressed as a percentage, that sets a benchmark for acceptable levels of system performance or reliability.

</details>

**2. How does a Service Level Agreement (SLA) usually differ from an SLO?**

- A) SLA measures internal performance only.
- B) SLA sets objectives without penalties.
- C) SLA is a formal contract that may involve penalties.
- D) SLA and SLO are essentially the same.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) SLA is a formal contract that may involve penalties.

A Service Level Agreement (SLA) is a formal contract between a provider and a customer, often including financial penalties if the agreed-upon service levels are not met.

</details>

**3. Why are both SLOs and SLAs important for service management?**

- A) They are only useful for financial audits.
- B) They provide benchmarks for internal guidance and enforce commitments, respectively.
- C) They are used interchangeably for setting service standards.
- D) They ensure that system updates are reached monthly.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) They provide benchmarks for internal guidance and enforce commitments, respectively.

SLOs guide internal goals by setting measurable targets, while SLAs enforce external commitments, often including penalties, thus maintaining trust between providers and customers.

</details>
