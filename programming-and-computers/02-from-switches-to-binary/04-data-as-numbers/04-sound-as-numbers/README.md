# Sound as Numbers

Sound is a wave traveling through air. To turn it digital, a computer measures the wave's position thousands of times per second. This is called **sampling**.

Each measurement becomes a number. CD-quality audio takes 44,100 samples every second. Play those numbers back in order through a speaker, and you hear the original sound. Just like images are grids of color numbers, audio is a long list of wave-position numbers.

## Quiz

**1. What is 'sampling' in the context of digital audio?**

- A) Compressing an audio file to save space
- B) Measuring the wave's position many times per second
- C) Adding sound effects to a recording
- D) Playing back a recorded sound

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) Measuring the wave's position many times per second

Sampling is the process of measuring a sound wave's position thousands of times per second. Each measurement becomes a number the computer can store.

</details>

**2. How many samples per second does CD-quality audio use?**

- A) 1,000
- B) 10,000
- C) 44,100
- D) 1,000,000

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) 44,100

CD-quality audio takes 44,100 samples per second. That high rate is enough to accurately capture sounds across the range of human hearing.

</details>

**3. What does a computer actually store to represent a sound?**

- A) A picture of the sound wave
- B) A long list of numbers representing wave positions
- C) The name of the instrument that made the sound
- D) A single average volume value

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) A long list of numbers representing wave positions

Digital audio is simply a long sequence of numbers, each one a measurement of the wave's position at a moment in time. Playing them back in order reconstructs the original sound.

</details>
