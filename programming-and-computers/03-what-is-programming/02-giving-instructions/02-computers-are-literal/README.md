# Computers Are Literal

Tell a friend "make a sandwich" and they'll figure it out. Tell a computer the same thing and it's stuck, it doesn't know what bread is, where the knife is, or what "make" means.

Computers have **zero common sense**. You have to spell out every detail: open the bag, take out two slices, pick up the knife, spread the butter. *What feels obvious to you is invisible to a machine.*

## Quiz

**1. What does it mean that computers are literal?**

- A) They understand general ideas and fill in the gaps
- B) They have zero common sense and need every detail spelled out
- C) They can guess your intent from context
- D) They only follow written English sentences

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) They have zero common sense and need every detail spelled out

Computers have zero common sense. What feels obvious to a human is invisible to a machine, so every detail must be spelled out.

</details>

**2. Why would 'make a sandwich' fail as an instruction to a computer?**

- A) Sandwiches are too complex to build
- B) The computer would make too many sandwiches
- C) The computer doesn't know what bread, a knife, or 'make' means without details
- D) The computer would refuse the task

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) The computer doesn't know what bread, a knife, or 'make' means without details

A computer has no background knowledge, so vague instructions leave it stuck. You have to describe every small action step by step.

</details>

**3. Which best shows how to instruct a literal computer?**

- A) 'Clean up the kitchen'
- B) 'Open the bag, take out two slices, pick up the knife, spread the butter'
- C) 'Do what I mean, not what I say'
- D) 'Figure out lunch for me'

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) 'Open the bag, take out two slices, pick up the knife, spread the butter'

Literal instructions break an action into explicit, ordered steps. That's the level of detail a computer needs to follow along.

</details>
