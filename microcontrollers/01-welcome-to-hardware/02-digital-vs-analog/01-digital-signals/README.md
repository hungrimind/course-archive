# Digital Signals

A **digital signal** has exactly two states: **high** (on) or **low** (off). There's nothing in between -- it's the same binary your code already thinks in.

High usually means 3.3V or 5V on the pin. Low means 0V. When you press a button, the pin reads high or low. When you turn on an LED, you set a pin high or low. *It's a light switch, not a dimmer.*

Most of what a microcontroller does -- reading buttons, blinking LEDs, sending data -- uses digital signals.

## True or False

**1. True or false:** A digital signal can represent three different voltage levels: low, medium, and high.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**False**: A digital signal has exactly two states -- high (on) and low (off). There is no in-between value.

</details>

**2. True or false:** When you press a button connected to a microcontroller, the pin reads either high or low.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: A button is a digital input. Pressing it changes the pin between two states (high and low), with nothing in between.

</details>

**3. True or false:** Most microcontroller tasks like reading buttons and blinking LEDs use digital signals.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**True**: Digital signals are the foundation of most microcontroller operations, including reading buttons, controlling LEDs, and sending data.

</details>
