# How PIR Sensors Work

A **PIR (Passive Infrared) sensor** detects motion by sensing changes in infrared radiation: the heat that people and animals naturally emit. It doesn't send out any signal of its own, which is why it's called *passive*.

The sensor has two infrared-sensitive slots behind a Fresnel lens. When a warm body moves across its field of view, one slot sees more IR than the other, and the sensor outputs **HIGH** on a single GPIO pin. No motion means the pin stays LOW.

Wiring is as simple as it gets: power, ground, and one data pin. No voltage dividers, no ADC, no libraries.

## Quiz

**1. Why is a PIR sensor called 'passive'?**

- A) Because it requires no power to operate
- B) Because it does not send out any signal of its own; it only detects existing infrared radiation
- C) Because it can only detect stationary objects
- D) Because it uses a passive resistor instead of an active transistor

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) Because it does not send out any signal of its own; it only detects existing infrared radiation

A PIR sensor is called passive because it does not emit any signal. It only senses changes in the infrared radiation already present in its environment.

</details>

**2. What does a PIR sensor actually detect to sense motion?**

- A) Visible light changes when something moves
- B) Sound waves reflected off moving objects
- C) Changes in infrared radiation caused by a warm body moving across its field of view
- D) Electromagnetic pulses generated by movement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) Changes in infrared radiation caused by a warm body moving across its field of view

PIR sensors have two infrared-sensitive slots behind a Fresnel lens. When a warm body moves, one slot sees more IR than the other, triggering a HIGH output.

</details>

**3. What extra components does a PIR sensor need to connect to a Raspberry Pi?**

- A) A voltage divider and an ADC
- B) An external library and a pull-up resistor
- C) None, just power, ground, and one data pin
- D) A Fresnel lens adapter and a signal amplifier

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) None, just power, ground, and one data pin

PIR sensors are wired very simply: just power, ground, and one data pin. No voltage dividers, ADC, or special libraries are required.

</details>
