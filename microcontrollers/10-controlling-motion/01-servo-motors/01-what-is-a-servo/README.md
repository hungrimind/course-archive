# What Is a Servo?

A **servo motor** moves to a specific angle and *holds* it there. Unlike a regular motor that just spins, a servo goes exactly where you tell it -- like a pointer on a dial.

Inside, it's three things working together: a small DC motor, a set of gears to slow it down and increase torque, and a **feedback circuit** that constantly checks the shaft's position. If something pushes the shaft off target, the feedback circuit corrects it automatically.

This makes servos ideal when you need precise angular positioning -- think robotic arms, camera gimbals, or steering mechanisms.

## Quiz

**1. What makes a servo motor different from a regular DC motor?**

- A) It spins faster than a DC motor
- B) It moves to a specific angle and holds it there
- C) It only rotates in one direction
- D) It does not require any electrical power

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- B) It moves to a specific angle and holds it there

A servo motor moves to a specific angle and holds that position, unlike a regular DC motor which just spins continuously.

</details>

**2. What three components work together inside a servo motor?**

- A) A battery, a speaker, and a sensor
- B) A stepper coil, a capacitor, and an LED
- C) A small DC motor, gears, and a feedback circuit
- D) A transformer, a relay, and a resistor

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) A small DC motor, gears, and a feedback circuit

A servo contains a small DC motor for rotation, gears to slow it down and increase torque, and a feedback circuit that constantly checks and corrects the shaft's position.

</details>

**3. What does the feedback circuit inside a servo do?**

- A) It converts AC power to DC power
- B) It generates the PWM signal for the motor
- C) It constantly checks the shaft's position and corrects it if pushed off target
- D) It limits the motor's maximum speed

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

- C) It constantly checks the shaft's position and corrects it if pushed off target

The feedback circuit monitors the shaft's position and automatically corrects it if something pushes the shaft off its target angle.

</details>
